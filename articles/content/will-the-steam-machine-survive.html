Will the Steam Machine Survive?

Vaminta
12/12/2025
HardwareSteam

Valve recently announced the Steam Machine, a cube shaped box that would look right at home under someone’s TV. It’s a small form factor PC which comes preinstalled with Steam OS, Valves own linux distro. This of course comes with a custom steam client version made to work well with a controller out of the box. It also includes settings to control the hardware itself, unlike the big picture mode on regular steam clients. Valve has previously unsuccessfully tried to launch Steam Machine devices, but it seems like they’ve learned from their previous mistakes, and this iteration takes a vastly different approach with hardware which is directly designed by Valve, in partnership with AMD.

Steam OS isn’t new - it has already been featured on the largely popular Steam Deck device, which allows you to play PC games on a device in the style of a Nintendo Switch.

Because most games are made to run on Windows, the Steam Machine will come with Proton. Proton is a translation layer (based on WINE) maintained by Valve which allows many Windows games to run on Linux. In fact, a lot of Windows games run faster on Linux under proton than they do running natively on Windows! It’s not perfect however, some games, such a popular online multiple games like Battlefield do not work under proton because they require kernel level anti-cheat which isn’t really possible to implement on Linux. There are alternative anti-cheat techniques available, but the Linux market isn’t big enough for game developers to invest in such an endeavor, especially when the results aren’t certain. Regardless, Proton is a groundbreaking tool when it comes to gaming on Linux, and many games run out-of-the-box with no adjustments required.

The device isn’t locked down either; behind the scenes, it’s a fully functional Linux desktop. Should you wish to suffer, you can even install Windows, although this certainly isn’t recommended.

What Valve didn’t announce was the price, at what could be argued the worst time to announce new hardware. The PC hardware market has been hit hard by huge demand for RAM, largely caused by sudden demand from AI companies wishing to bolster their compute centres. This sudden increase in demand has stretched supply thin and caused prices to rocket, making PC building prohibitively expensive for individual consumers. Recently, Micron, a major RAM manufacturer has announced it will be discontinuing “Crucial”, its brand aimed at individual PC builders. Micron’s website states that they will continue to ship “Crucial” products until the end of fiscal Q2 (February) 2026. After which Crucial branded memory will cease being sold at “key retailers, e-tailers and distributors worldwide.” This is presumably so it can focus its efforts on selling directly to (AI) businesses which tend to place large orders, and will happily purchase everything they’re making, at least for now. What’s even worse is that some analysts have noted that this shortage could last for years, although this is difficult to accurately predict. We can only hope that Valve has already agreed on a price for RAM from its suppliers. If it hasn’t, the Steam Machine’s manufacturing cost could be greatly affected.

But what does this mean for the Steam Machine? I believe that the success of the Steam Machine will be heavily affected by the price. Although Valve has previously said that it will be priced like a “PC”, it can’t be denied that the hardware will be closely compared with the existing consoles on the market such as the PS5 and Xbox Series X. Based on the specifications which Valve released, it can be estimated that the Steam machine will have performance between the Xbox Series S and Series X/ PS5. Much like the Steam Deck, the Steam Machine will include a perfectly functional Linux Desktop which will allow you to use it for work, or watching videos, etc.
In order to be an attractive purchase, I believe the Steam Machine will need to be priced around a PS5 Pro, which currently sits around 700 GBP, or 750 USD. Anymore than 800, and the Steam Machine will start to lose people looking to have an affordable option in PC gaming.

The Steam Machine would be perfect for those who want to jump into PC gaming without the cost or complexities that often come with it. Or those who already have a large Steam library and want to be able to easily take their games to the living room TV. It will not receive all the exclusives that come to the PS5, at least not for a while, but Steam benefits from its huge library which dwarfs any library belonging to any other console. Recently, Sony has been porting some of its exclusive titles to Steam, and Microsoft also has their titles on PC making it a good platform to experience games from both sides of the gaming market. Additionally, Steam is known for its frequent sales which often makes buying games on PC a lot more affordable than buying on console. Not to mention, multiplayer access is free as PC is an open platform so you’re not forced into paying any subscription fees. This coupled with the fact that it’s a completely functional PC means that it can be priced slightly higher than an equivalent console.

It remains to be seen what the final price for Steam Machine will be, and what impact that will have on its success.
