LINE: Japan’s No. 1 Messaging App That’s in Serious Need of a Diet!

Vaminta
30/06/2026
SoftwareMobile

If you’re staying in Japan for any significant period of time, one of the most important apps you’ll want to install on your phone is LINE, from LY. Without LINE, you’ll find it difficult to keep in touch with Japanese people in Japan. This is because LINE is, by far, the biggest call and messaging platform in Japan. You’ll be hard pressed to find any Japanese people who use WhatsApp, SMS or other platforms.

So that begs the question: what makes LINE so good? My answer is simple: nothing. In my honest opinion, LINE is inferior to its overseas equivalents in almost every aspect. Here’s why.

Paid Content

LINE includes a store which allows you to purchase stickers for real money. However, to complicate things, you’ll first need to purchase intermediary LINE coins which you can then spend on stickers and other content. So what are stickers? Stickers are essentially themed image packs. For example, for 100 LINE Coins, you can purchase a pokemon sticker pack which contains 40 images. For reference, 100 LINE Coins is equal to 300 yen!

There is a huge library of packs available from large and small brands, such as Pokemon, Disney, Sanrio and Miffy. You can even find packs for small brands you wouldn’t normally expect. After you’ve purchased stickers you can send them in your chats like any other image. Essentially you’ve purchased some pictures you can send to other people easily, without the trouble of having to download them for free off the internet.

Stickers are far from the only paid content you can purchase on the app. There’s also themes, which as the name suggests, allows you to change the theme of the app. I suppose this is more interesting in the sense that it provides a feature that otherwise doesn’t exist in most other apps, albeit very gimmicky. And Instagram does allow you to customise the theme in chats now.

A Payload for Ads

Given the aforementioned paid content, LINE must be doing quite well on the financial side of things. However, why stop there when you could also include banner ads throughout the app? That's right, LINE features ads spanning the width of the screen above your most recently messaged contact. Of course, there are ads all over the app, but this to me is the most egregious.

It’s not just banners either. Many businesses in Japan will offer you incentives to add them on LINE, whether that be discounts or some aforementioned sticker packs. In return, they can spam you with ads in the form of messages as if it was coming from any other of your contacts. There is a way to mute chats but the fact that this is normalised just seems odd to me.

Bloat Galore

The sheer amount of bloat in this app would make any laptop manufacturer jealous. The aforementioned stickers and themes are only scratching the surface. LY seems to want to position LINE as the everything app, much like Musk wants to do with X (formerly Twitter).

Upon opening the app, you will see five tabs along the bottom, only one of which (the chats tab) you’ll actually want to use. The other four, labelled home, shopping, news and MINI are virtually useless.

The Home tab is mostly home to random news and a never ending stream of ads. Shopping is for buying tat and virtual tickets to places, nothing you couldn’t do better anywhere else. News is just a mediocre news aggregation page. Mini is a launcher for mini apps, literally referred to as “Mini Apps”. At this point we’ve officially reached the “apps within apps” stage.

But wait, there’s more! Voom is LINE’s version of instagram reels - again an imitation of the superior slop viewer. Agent i is the name given to the seemingly obligatory chatbot inclusion. AI friends is yet another AI chatbot that roleplays as both historical and fictional characters. LINE GAME is also an app within an app for playing a random assortment of games. There’s also a bitcoin trader, rewards club, point club and a whole load of other… stuff.

Alright, I’ve had enough, let’s move on.

Missing Features

LINE has one primary job, and it still does it badly. Features that have become commonplace in other apps are nowhere to be seen here. For example, live location sharing. I’ve found this to be such a useful feature on whatsapp. Instead of sharing a location and having to wait in place, you can instead share your live location and both be able to head towards each other. Of course it’s also useful just to let your contact know where you are. However, LINE only allows you to share a static location.

View-once photos are also a nice feature to have. Sometimes you don’t want photos lingering on your, or your recipient's phone. Once a photo has been viewed, it gets deleted. Whatsapp also prevents screenshots, however you shouldn’t completely rely on this since these measures can be bypassed.

Unfortunately, many basic features are locked behind a paywall (perhaps unsurprisingly at this point). If you want to back-up your chats, unsend messages “discreetly” or even change the font, you’ll need to pay up. The price is 650 JPY/ month. That makes it more expensive than an Amazon prime membership!

Slow and Buggy

Perhaps due to all of the bloat, the app is rather heavy, and takes significantly longer to load than apps like WhatsApp or Signal. This issue only gets worse on more budget friendly phones, where LINE can appear to have completely frozen on the splash screen for several seconds.

Additionally, in my experience, bugs are also rather common. Thankfully they are rather minor, but their inclusion and frequent occurrence just makes using the app that bit more unpleasant than it already was. Even basic tasks like trying to scroll up in an emoji selector can really test my patience.

Limited Desktop Support

WhatsApp, Signal, Messenger and Instagram all have wide support for various operating systems whether by means of a web app or native application. However, LINE only supports Windows 10+ or Mac OS, which is slightly disappointing.
There are ways to get access to LINE on Linux, but it requires installing the Chrome extension which isn’t exactly ideal.

Only Japan

Many of LINE's (anti)features are actually only accessible with a Japanese phone number. If you log-in with a phone number from any other country, you’ll see a vastly slimmed down version of the app. Gone are many useless features you’ll never use anyway, but also gone are the ads!

Perhaps signing up with a foreign number gets you a better experience. However, there is a catch. Some open chats are region locked, and you can only join if your account is tied to a Japanese number.

Conclusion

I’m often left wondering how LINE ever became Japan’s primary communications app. Even if it was popular in the past, it’s impressive that it remains a key leader in the Japanese market in the face of fierce competition from alternatives.
In my limited experience, Instagram is also popular in Japan. Just some years ago, Instagram's messenger was woefully lacking, but relatively recently it has greatly improved. Whenever possible, I’ll opt to send messages on Instagram rather than LINE. The less I can use LINE the better.
